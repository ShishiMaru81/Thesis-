Fine-tuning is widely used to adapt large language models to specialised domains, and is typically evaluated on task accuracy alone. We show that this evaluation misses a failure mode that fine-tuning actively creates.

We built a bilingual Bengali–English question-answering system for irrigation advisory, targeting runtime-conditioned queries whose answers depend on live evapotranspiration, rainfall, crop stage, and field size, and therefore cannot be retrieved from any document. Four models (Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-NeMo-12B, Phi-3.5-mini, Phi-4-mini) were fine-tuned across three seeds each on a leakage-audited corpus derived from BARC crop calendars, and evaluated on a hardened 422-item test set containing adversarial misspellings, unit ambiguity, and incomplete queries, of which 362 items were scored.

Fine-tuning installed domain knowledge that no base model possessed: all four scored 0.000 F1 on FAO-56 crop coefficients and the tiered effective-rainfall rule before training, and 0.542–0.761 and 0.930–0.999 respectively after. Exact numeric accuracy (within ±0.1 mm) rose from 0.0–3.4% to 38.8–69.3%; within ±1 mm it rose from 8.6–18.1% to 80.7–98.9%.

The same training produced no clarification behaviour in any of the twelve fine-tuned cells (four models × three seeds). When a required parameter was absent, every fine-tuned model fabricated a value and computed with it 100% of the time, while correctly refusing the out-of-scope questions it was tested on 100% of the time — the difference being that abstention appeared in the training corpus (3.6% of rows) and clarification did not. The base models also invented values (97.1–100%), but their output often ran to the 512-token limit without finishing (13.2–66.2% of these responses). Fine-tuned output never did (0%) and instead delivered a short, well-formatted irrigation volume computed from the invented value.

Adding 300 clarification examples (seed 42) raised clarification, measured as asking for the missing parameter by name, to 29.4–38.2% across all four architectures (33.8–50.0% when any request for more information is counted). On Llama-3.1-8B, a post-hoc parameter detector raised combined safe handling to 83.8%; this figure is in-sample, because the detector's rules were extended with the test categories in view. We further find the Bengali–English gap clearest in text generation, where English led on chrF++ by 6–13 points in every model. On exact numeric accuracy English also led in all four models, by 0.6–13.2 points; at ±1 mm no consistent direction emerged.
